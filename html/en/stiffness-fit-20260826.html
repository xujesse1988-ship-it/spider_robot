T1 step 1: parallel-spring model fit of the handover segment — closing the model loop, gate decision and a new lever

2026-08-26. Data = per-leg handover segments of the six B/C reports of the n=3 Latin square (54+54 points, C2 after the sync correction, see deviation ① in the n=3 report); tool software/logs_analysis/fit_stiffness.py. Model: Δbj,r = c0·δj·(kj − Σkisi)·(1+γ(r−1)) + common-mode term, Σk̂=1.

0. Conclusions at a glance

  • Model loop closed: within group B, 18 cells (×3 replicates) fit with RMSE 0.85 mm (signal 6.8–12.6 mm), leave-one-replicate-out CV-RMSE 0.99 mm; k̂ vs. the k̂ converted from the 08-24 slopes, Pearson r=0.938.
  • k̂ (B+C joint, M8): L1 0.235 / R1 0.217 / L3 0.143 / R3 0.138 / R2 0.138 / L2 0.129 — L1 is still the stiffest but the L1−R1 gap is far smaller than in the slope table (0.018–0.028 vs. 0.07); the ranking is stable across models.
  • The decomposition that overturns the story: B handover segment 60.2 mm = stiffness-redistribution term ~6.7 + common-mode term ~55.5; C 39.7 = 7.7 + 30.0. About 90% of the loss is "common-mode sinking at every handover", not stiffness inequality; the weights (C) cut the common-mode rate by ~46% (ρ 0.80→0.43 mm/s).
  • Gate decision: the original design D (stiffness-aware share) does not go on the wall — the share only moves the redistribution term, gaining ~3 mm for B and ~4.4 mm for C; the expected "handover segment 60→10" does not hold. The planned worries about L1 having no solution / everything pushed onto R1 largely disappear with the corrected k̂ (residual coefficient only 0.019), but the lever itself is too small.
  • New lever D′ (software only): the common-mode term ∝ handover-window duration (3.2±0.4 s, mostly the apportioning at δ/10 mm·s⁻¹). Raising the apportioning rate 10→20 mm/s predicts the C handover segment 39.7→~25 mm (C total 72.9→~58). ⚠ Window duration is perfectly collinear with δ, so "time creep" vs. "per-mm loss" cannot be told apart offline — D′ is at the same time a mechanism-discrimination experiment: if faster apportioning lowers the loss it is time creep (12 mm for free); if not, it is per-mm loss (rewrite the model); both outcomes are valuable.

1. Prerequisite: C2 sync correction

While checking the nine decomposition tables before fitting, the per-leg pattern of C2 (0825pm/C) was found shifted by one slot as a whole (a spurious "big R2/L2 drop"), traced to the step rescue locking onto the wrong tooth of a periodic comb (intra-round spacing 17.5–18.5 s highly periodic; teeth one slot apart differ in R² by <0.001). Two independent anchors fix the true tooth + a clipped-window marker fit gives off=59.34 (R²=0.99, amplitude 4.23 mm = transfer ratio 42.3%, independently cross-checked); the report was regenerated and the C column of the n=3 main table refreshed (total conserved 70.2→70.5, split and per-leg attribution corrected, SD tightened; transfer-ratio samples 8→9). Full story and lessons: n=3 report deviation ①; new tool marker_off_clip.py (general clipped-window marker sync).

3. Out-of-sample falsification on C and joint fit

Predicting C from the B-fitted parameters + rotation-weight shares: predicted total 61.8 vs. measured 39.5 mm — "the common-mode term is independent of share/condition" is falsified on the spot by C: the weights not only dilute the vent residual, they also cut the handover-segment common-mode term by ~20 mm. What "the sag concentrates in the stiffest L1/R1" really consists of: the creep base is common-mode (from ~2.4 mm per window), but when a soft leg is unloaded the redistribution term is negative (body rises slightly) and just cancels the base — soft-leg windows look ≈0; stiff-leg windows = positive redistribution + base stacked. Stiffness inequality determines the shape of the distribution (±2–5 mm); the magnitude (~55 mm) is set by creep. The mechanism statement of Finding 1 must be rewritten accordingly; the round-by-round growth = γ (redistribution) and β′ (creep rate) both climbing, from the same source as the current climb, with no saturation seen within 3 rounds (an 8–10-round long run is still needed).

5. Gate decision and next steps

The original design D (stiffness-aware share), judged by "the model gives the number first; below expectation, it does not go on the wall": does not go on the wall. Only the redistribution term is movable by the share: B 6.7→3.5, C 7.7→3.3 mm (L1 still has no solution but the residual coefficient is only 0.019; the planned worry "everything on R1 hits torque saturation" disappears with the k̂ correction — R1 no longer needs the full share). A gain of ~3–4 mm is not worth a wall condition; the share solution is kept as a merged micro-optimization after D′.

New experiment D′: handover apportioning rate 10→20 mm/s (condition C, one variable at a time). Prediction (time-creep hypothesis): C handover segment 39.7→~25 mm, C total 72.9→~58 mm; if it does not fall, the verdict is "per-mm loss" and the model is rewritten — mechanism discrimination and performance gain in one. Risk and guards: faster = less quasi-static; protocol §1 criterion 4 (smooth handover segment) and the leak-rescue pause stay as usual; pty/floor smoke test first. Companion: the T4a hanging-weight stiffness experiment can measure the creep rate directly (hang a load, rest, watch the y(t) slope vs. load, turning ρ from a fitted parameter into an independent measurement and giving absolute k).

6. Limitations (honestly)

  • T_window and δ are perfectly collinear (T≈0.5+δ/10): M8's "× duration" and "× apportioned amount" are equivalent offline; D′ is the only means of discrimination; until then the "creep rate" is a phenomenological parameter.
  • c0 (1.0–2.6 depending on model) absorbs unknown gains (transfer ratio / unit accounting); k̂ drifts ±0.03 between models, but the ranking and the L1−R1 gap are stable; absolute stiffness awaits T4a.
  • The mechanism behind ρ_C<ρ_B (stress nonlinearity vs. cup freshness) is undetermined — a share-perturbation experiment would be needed to separate them; not blocking for now.
  • No γ/β′ saturation visible within 3 rounds; long-distance extrapolation is invalid, to be filled by a long-run experiment.
  • Data after the C2 correction (see §1); group B unaffected.
