2. Quantification conventions (newly fixed for this report)

  • Time sync: ±10 mm lean-marker shape fit, off = 125.82 / 62.58 / 58.40 s, R² = 0.99 / 0.98 / 0.99.
  • Scale: YYNMOS-8 board long edge, physical 93.0 mm (P4 bay hardware record) ÷ pixel length of that edge in frame 0 of each run (315.2/314.5/314.8 px, sub-pixel edge fit) = 0.2951 / 0.2957 / 0.2954 mm/px, taken as 0.295 (±0.5%). The short-edge scale (0.322) is foreshortened by the board's ~24° out-of-plane tilt and is discarded. The paper-ruler plane gives 0.326 mm/px, used only as a projection cross-check (group A endpoint 641 px: ruler-plane reading difference 20.9 cm ↔ body plane 18.9 cm, ratio = depth ratio of the two planes 0.905, self-consistent). The ±10 mm marker cannot serve as an absolute scale — it measures the actual displacement (see §6 Finding 2).
  • Tracking health: static ref score min 0.996, camera shake 2.2–4.8 px @4K (≈ baseline convention <1.2 px), good; median body_score 0.645/0.761/0.683, below the baseline convention of 0.8 (board tilt 4–8° + fine 4K texture is unfriendly to axis-aligned NCC); but the per-leg pattern reproduces 08-20 across days (see §4), so the structure is trustworthy.

6. Two paper-grade new findings

Finding 1: the main battlefield of the residual slip moves from the rupture instant to the handover apportioning segment, concentrated in the two stiffest legs.
Group B L1 handover-segment displacement grows round by round 6.8→10.3→12.6 mm (climbing with the steady-state stored energy), R1 6.6→8.2→10.2; the other four legs ≈0–1.5 mm. Mechanism: "six-leg command mean unchanged ⇒ body does not move" only holds when all six legs have equal stiffness; the elastic equilibrium is a stiffness-weighted average, so moving load from the stiffest L1/R1 (bounce-vs-δ slopes 0.57/0.39, the largest of all) to the five softer legs necessarily sinks the body. Weights (C) cut this segment by 27% (60.7→44.3 mm).
Engineering meaning: a next-generation "stiffness-aware share" — solving Σk·d=0 for each stance leg's received share — could bring the handover-segment displacement to zero, corresponding to about 57–62% headroom on the B/C residual slip; it is also the experimental basis for the paper's three-generation story "equal split → rotation weights → stiffness-aware".
Finding 2: command→actual compliance transfer ratio ~40–46% (whole robot, measured directly without sensors).
Fitted amplitude of the ±10 mm sync marker 15.45/13.90/13.35 px (R² 0.987/0.982/0.994) × 0.295 mm/px = actual body displacement 4.56/4.10/3.94 mm, i.e. only 45.6%/41.0%/39.4% of the commanded displacement reaches the body; the rest is eaten by leg-chain compliance. Three independent measurements cross-validate; the slight decline with voltage (7.88→7.51 V) suggests a link to servo torque, still to be verified. Same storyline as the 08-19 press reaction bending the leg chain and the Appendix A spring model; first whole-robot quantitative number.

9. Conclusions and next steps

Main conclusion: three runs on the same day under the same conditions — the zero-force handover reduces three-round total slip from 18.5 cm to 10.7 cm (−42%), and adding rotation weights to 7.2 cm (−61%); rupture-instant displacement −81%/−90%, and every per-leg bounce in group C is ≤2.1 mm. Yesterday's quick test (estimated δ table, 12→7 cm) and today's calibrated-table results confirm each other. Data precision (sync R²≥0.98, scale ±0.5%, cross-day reproduction 0.5 mm) reaches the paper-usable line.
