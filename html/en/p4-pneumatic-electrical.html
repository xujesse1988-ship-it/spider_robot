Electrical wiring of the pneumatic subsystem

Only the electrical set that serves the pneumatics is drawn: the 12 V power chain boost → MOSFET → 6 valves + 2 pump positions (1 fitted in V0), the Pi's 8-channel control chain, and the 5 V sampling chain of the 7 pressure sensors. Servos / Servo2040 / relay are not part of the pneumatics, see html/en/p4-system-diagram.html. This diagram goes down to terminal level — which wire goes into which screw terminal, with terminal names taken verbatim from the back-side silkscreen in images/YYNMOS-8_back.jpg. Two deliberate design differences from existing documents: the dual-rail supply in Fig. 1 and pump "+" not via the board DC+, OUT7+/OUT8+ left open in Fig. 3.

Pump count: this diagram draws both pump positions, but V0 fits a single pump. The basis is the weight-saving measure in P4-BAY-DESIGN: "first flight with one pump, second position kept". Wherever the diagram says pump B / channel 8 / IN8± / OUT8±, it stays empty in V0 — terminals and wire positions are kept, the second pump plugs straight in later, and nothing else changes. The current budget lists both cases; the dual-rail conclusion holds for a single pump too (see the check below the budget table).

  • Conclusion: even with only one pump, do not merge onto one module. The XL6009's current limit is on the input side (switch and inductor sit in the input loop); the chip's switch current limit is 4 A peak, and the inductor ripple stacks on top of the mean; the SS34 on the module is a 3 A part and the small inductor typically saturates at 3–4 A. Against the table: at nominal voltage the steady 3.2 A is already at the limit, in the second half of the battery it is over, and the pump start-up will hit current limiting and hiccup. The "3 A" in the BOM is the module's nominal input rating, not its 12 V output capability — boosting 7.4 V to 12 V, that corresponds to only about 1.5 A of output. With separate rails the pump rail carries 0.8 A and the valve rail 0.9 A, both easy.
  • Failure direction of a shared-rail collapse (the old version had this backwards; corrected here): this project's valves are normally closed, powered = vent (VALVE_ON_LEVEL=0 in adhesion.py), so an unpowered valve does not draw from this rail at all — a voltage dip cannot release a foot that is holding vacuum. The real failure chains are two: ① a venting valve drops out → the foot that should be released re-grips halfway while the gait state machine thinks it is free; lifting = tearing; ② while dropping out the valve is half-open and leaks air into the shared vacuum manifold, while the undervolted pump loses output → tank pressure rises → all six feet weaken together. ② is the path to "everyone loses suction". The conclusion that rails must be split stands, but this is the reasoning chain.
  • A single pump needs the split more, not less: with two pumps each has a low duty cycle and starts rarely; a single pump must top up 6 feet + tank alone, its duty cycle rises and it starts more often, so the probability of a pump-start window overlapping a valve-action window goes up. "One pump fewer, so the rails can be merged" points the wrong way.
  • The battery is shared with the servos: P2 measured 2–4 A sustained and ~12 A transient for the whole robot (servo side). Whenever the servos move the battery terminal voltage dips, and the boost's input-current demand jumps at the same moment — the "second half 6.6 V" row in the table is this case, not something that appears only when the battery is drained.
  • The fuse rating changes with pump count: one pump referred to the battery side is 2.1–3.2 A steady, so a 5 A fuse has ~1.6× margin, fine. With the second pump added the steady state rises to 3.1–4.8 A, hugging 5 A and fatiguing the fuse; then switch to a 7.5–10 A time-delay (slow-blow) type and update row 1 of the quick reference.
  • Add a 470–1000 µF / 25 V electrolytic across the pump-rail output (right at the XL6009 output terminals) to absorb the start-up surge; 220 µF is enough on the valve rail. Do not reduce it for a single pump — the surge is one pump's own start-up characteristic, independent of how many are fitted. Mind the polarity.
  • Estimates come from the P1 measurement "1 valve + 1 pump ≈ 1 A" (P1-GUIDE.md); the 2–3× pump surge ratio is a rule of thumb, not yet measured in this project. On arrival measure each item with a clamp meter and back-fill this table — it decides wire gauges, fuse rating and whether rails can be merged; it is not decoration. The first thing to measure is the pump start-up current waveform (clamp meter peak-hold, or a 0.1 Ω shunt + oscilloscope).
  • If the floor tripod gait allows 3 feet to release at once, re-check the valve rail at 3 valves ≈1.1 A (still within margin with split rails); a merged single-pump rail would then peak at ~3.6 A on 12 V, 6.9 A on the input side at nominal voltage, 1.7× the 4 A limit, even less possible.
Three deliberate differences from existing documents (this diagram is the terminal-level implementation; in a conflict this diagram wins, and please back-fill P4-BAY-DESIGN)
  1. Dual-rail supply: p4-system-diagram.html and P4-BAY-DESIGN §2.2 draw "XL6009 ×2 side by side → MOSFET board power in", which reads like two modules paralleled onto one 12 V. Boost modules cannot be paralleled directly (output voltages can never be exactly equal; the higher one carries the whole load, the other idles or even back-feeds). This diagram changes to #1 dedicated to the pump, #2 dedicated to the board DC+ (valves draw from the on-board OUTn+ bus), each independent, sharing only the power ground. The bay layout does not change — the two still sit side by side, only their outputs go separate ways.
  2. Pump "+" not via board DC+, OUT7+ / OUT8+ open: the back-side silkscreen shows each channel has its own OUTn+ / OUTn−, with OUTn+ internally paralleled onto DC+. So valves are easiest — two wires across OUTn+ / OUTn− and done; pumps the reverse: OUT7+/OUT8+ must stay open and "+" takes the pump rail alone, otherwise the two rails are shorted together inside the board by the DC+ bus and the split is wasted. This is the premise that makes the split work, and the only easy mistake in the wiring.
  3. Pull-down resistors across INn± + 5 A fuse: two new items absent from existing documents. The board's inputs are 8 optocoupler-isolated pairs (INn+ / INn−); the resistor goes across those two terminals of the same channel (4.7k–10k, 6.2k fitted), as a backstop for the reset default pull-up of GPIO5/6 (see the Fig. 3 footnote); the fuse is dictated by the short-circuit energy of a 2S LiPo — the segment after the XT60 and before the boosts is the only place in the whole robot without any current limiting.
Assembly and inspection checklist (in order; do not proceed until each step passes)
  1. Four bench measurements before the board goes on: all done 2026-08-11, results in the table below Fig. 2. Flyback diodes confirmed on all eight channels; this step is closed.
  2. Set both XL6009 unloaded, and deliberately leave a voltage difference: connect each alone to the battery, unloaded, set #2 valve rail 12.2 V / #1 pump rail 11.8 V (not both 12.0 V), power off, label "#1 pump rail / #2 valve rail", then connect loads. The direction of the difference is deliberate: the on-board flyback diode points "pump − → valve rail", so once the pump rail exceeds the valve rail by one Vf (measured silicon ≈0.59 V) it leaks current through the motor winding into the valve rail, and the pump hums or turns slowly. The 0.1 V error of hand-setting two modules would not reach that threshold by itself; the 0.4 V is a guard against the potentiometer drifting with pump vibration. Acceptance: with the pump off + 3 valves energized (valve rail at its lowest, pump rail at its highest) measure both rails; it passes only if the valve rail is still higher than the pump rail.
  3. Control first, no loads: Pi ↔ IN1+ to IN8+ + the paralleled IN− signal ground + 8 pull-downs (6.2k fitted). With the Pi powered but the program not running, measure each INn+ against INn−; it passes at <0.8 V — this is the only window in which the GPIO5/6 reset pull-up actually takes effect. With the Pi unpowered the internal pull-ups are off, that measurement always passes and proves nothing.
  4. Jog unloaded: connect 12 V, no valves or pumps, jog each GPIO channel and watch the LED and the OUTn− to OUTn+ voltage flip (off ≈12 V, on ≈0 V). Connect loads only after all eight pass.
  5. Loads one channel at a time: the 6 valves first (listen for the click and confirm the air-path direction at the same time: cup to vacuum when unpowered, to air when powered), then the pump (V0 pump A only → channel 7). Re-check the total current after each channel.
  6. Measured power-loss hold test: with the whole robot attached, pull the master switch directly; all six feet must stay attached for ≥60 s (this is the whole point of the VALVE_ON_LEVEL=0 wiring). If this fails, no wall climbing.
  7. Sensor chain last: i2cdetect -y 1 shows 0x48/0x49; with 7 sensors open to air the readings should agree at ≈0.25 V. Read again with the pump running; only if the 7 readings jitter by <±1 kPa is the separate-comb routing done right.
  8. After everything passes, re-check vcgencmd get_throttled for 0x0, and back-fill the measured currents, terminal positions and GPIO map into this page and adhesion.py.
