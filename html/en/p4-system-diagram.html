Whole-robot component connection diagram (electrical + pneumatic)

This is the connection topology of the P4 whole-robot target state. Fig. 1 electrical: the 2S battery goes through the XT60 master switch into three branches (servos 7.4 V direct / Pi independent 5 V / pneumatics 12 V); control signals run over USB, GPIO and I2C; Fig. 1b relay: terminal-level wiring of the servo power master switch; Fig. 2 pneumatics: two pumps + vacuum tank + 1-in-6-out manifold form the vacuum bus, with 6 identical branches to the foot cups.

⚠ Must test before assembly: the port semantics of off-the-shelf 0520B valves are often the opposite (unpowered = exhaust open). During assembly, measure every valve's unpowered behavior on the bench (which two ports connect when unpowered); if reversed, connect the vacuum to the valve's "exhaust port" instead. Taking an "unpowered = vent" wiring onto the wall means power loss = fall.
Key rules
  • Pi powered separately: a stalled-servo voltage dip must never reboot the Pi — a reboot mid-climb = a fall. After assembly, confirm no undervoltage with vcgencmd get_throttled.
  • Every joint = a leak point: 4 mm barbs + cable ties throughout, sealed with epoxy, not hot glue.
  • No breadboards / Dupont plugs in P4 — vibration = intermittent disconnects = state-machine misjudgment; divider board soldered on perfboard + JST / soldered wires.
  • Check valves go between pump and tank, direction "tank→pump" only — prevents back-leak through the pump diaphragm after the pump stops.
  • Tank = vacuum battery: new foot attaches in a second, smooths peaks, buffers a pump failure — network volume 50× larger than bare tubing, leak decay 50× slower.
  • Iron rule of the gait: never release the next foot before the new foot's pressure is confirmed ≤ −30 kPa (always five feet supporting).
