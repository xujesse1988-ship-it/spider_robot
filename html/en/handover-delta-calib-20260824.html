Zero-force handover δ three-run calibration: bounce ≠ stored energy

2026-08-24 · glass wall, per-leg lift/land in place (window order R3→L1→R2→L3→R1→L2, about two and a half rounds each) · code dd778e6 · iPhone 16 Pro Max 4K@30 HEVC originals · NCC tracking of the body PCB + static reference on hazard-stripe tape (hand-held shake of 22–25 px removed) · scale: cm graph paper 0.358 → glass plane 0.318 mm/px (the mm convention of this page, ±10% systematic uncertainty on absolute values; the δ* extrapolation is independent of scale) · conclusions already landed: 2990449 (δ parse cap 25→45 + calibration method changed to linear extrapolation)

TL;DR: the three-run comparison δ=0 / 12 / 24 shows the per-leg vent bounce falling strictly linearly with δ, but at the cap δ=24 every leg still bounces down — extrapolating to zero bounce needs δ* ≈ 28–42 mm/leg (front legs ~40), all above the 25 mm parse cap of the time. The root is a semantic error in the calibration: the bounce at the vent instant is only the reading after the stored energy has been caught in parallel by the other five legs (≈1/5–1/8 of the stored energy); what δ must give back is the stored energy itself — so the "bounce×0.8" starting table was systematically 4–8× low. Total slip per round 66 → 50 → 39 mm: right direction, insufficient dose. The handover mechanism itself works (body displacement during the handover segment essentially zero, criterion 4 passed).

2. Data quality and two errata

Erratum 1: the ab_quant activity-contrast sync was wrong for all three runs. Run A was off by one lift/land interval (off 83.25 vs. true 76.14, contrast 0.1, clearly unlocked); runs B/C were off by 5–6 slots (82.05/93.70 vs. 47.24/53.14) — and B/C's contrasts of 4.8/8.6 looked healthy: the lift/land rhythm is quasi-periodic, so an integer-slot offset scores just as well. Root cause = the ↑↓ lean sync marker of protocol §4.3 was skipped + the recordings had no quiet reference segment before the first lift. All numbers on this page are based on a step-basis regression re-sync (y(t) fitted by least squares to steps at the 18 rel instants, R² = 0.9998 / 0.9997 / 0.9995 for the three runs, clearly separated from the second-best peak; script ~/ab_cache/redecompose.py). Next experiment: do the sync marker without fail; adding a step fallback / manual --off to the pipeline is a separate item.
Erratum 2: no tape ruler for scale calibration; three-plane geometry used instead. The background is a 1 m tall sheet of cm graph paper (uniform 27.9 px/cm top to bottom → paper plane 0.358 mm/px), but the paper sits about 9 cm behind the glass, with parallax relative to the cup plane. Closing the loop with the lens FOV + joint height differences gives glass plane ≈0.318, body plane ≈0.27 mm/px. The mm values on this page use the glass-plane convention (comparable with the 08-20 practice of a tape ruler on the glass), ±10% systematic uncertainty on absolute values; δ* = intercept/|slope| scales numerator and denominator alike, so the extrapolation is independent of calibration — the hardest number of this experiment.

Tracking health: median body_score 0.85 / 0.93 / 0.88, ref_score min ≥ 0.67; camera shake (ref_y range) 22–25 px is hand-held grade and was removed frame by frame against the static reference — after removal the trajectory is explained by a pure step basis to R²≥0.9995, which is itself proof that the tracking is clean.

The slopes differ 4× between legs (L1 −0.57 vs. R2 −0.13) = leg-chain stiffness differences: this also rules out the conversion "δ = 5× own bounce" under the uniform-stiffness approximation — the correct calibration is per-leg linear extrapolation, each leg growing its own conversion factor (L1 needs only 1.7× its own bounce, R2 needs 8×). The first-lift R3 bounce ≈0 in round 1 matches the model's prediction "zero preload at landing, no stored energy at the first lift".

4. Slip per round and trajectories

Clean dose-response: round-2 total slip 65.6 → 50.0 → 38.7 mm (−24% / −41%). Right direction, but still far from the protocol's <10 mm/round pass line — δ=24 returned only 58–86% of each leg's stored energy.

The within-round shape is consistent across the three runs (round 1 includes the first-round ramp, round 3 lacks the last L2 in each), so the decrease is the effect of δ, not day-to-day drift; run A's shape matches the 08-20 baseline (L1/R1 largest, total 66 vs. 74, −11% within day-to-day range).

Fig. 3 · Body trajectories of the three runs (cumulative slip mm, down positive; after reference correction). Top ticks = seal-rupture instants (rel). All three are clean staircases: displacement happens almost entirely at the rupture instants, and δ only makes each step shorter — the 08-20 conclusion that slow venting does not help still stands.

5. Three mechanism checks

  1. Linearity ⇒ stored energy does not change with δ. The share handed to the stance legs is returned exactly during the rotation (geometric closure), so increasing δ does not lower the system's wind-up level; it only deducts one more share at the vent instant — the collinearity of the three points is exactly this prediction.
  2. Step-basis R²≥0.9995 ⇒ displacement still concentrates at the rupture instant. Including the δ=24 run: the handover changes the step height, not the moment when displacement occurs.
  3. Body displacement during the handover segment ≈ 0 (criterion 4 passed, with one explained exception). Run B all ≤0.7 mm; run C all ≤2 mm except L1 at +2.4 to +3.3 mm (on the protocol's 3 mm line). L1's slight sinking is the inherent physics of unequal stiffness: when the stiffest leg of the whole robot is unloaded, the k-weighted mechanical equilibrium must shift a little toward the soft side, growing linearly with δ — not an apportioning-direction bug; in the reading it is waved through as the same kind as a "small step on the uphill side".

6. Current: the ratchet persists, and the original v2 idea "look for a plateau in total current" is refuted by the data

Total current (driver-board ADC, quantization 81 mA/LSB): all three runs start at rest around ~0.76 A and end at rest at 1.65 / 2.39 / 2.90 A without coming back down — if δ is not sufficient, internal stress accumulates as before (absolute values across runs are disturbed by battery state, qualitative only).

R1#1 (unloading a front leg: falls to a "plateau") R2#3 (unloading a middle leg: the sum rises instead)

Fig. 4 · Bus current inside two handover windows of run C (pump-off samples, 0.5 s sampling; t=0 handover start, dashed = δ=24 fully apportioned, vent). R1 "falls to a plateau" as soon as δ≈10 mm is apportioned — but the video quantification shows R1 still has a residual 6.9 mm bounce at δ=24 (δ*=41): the knee in the total current is the balance point "unloading gain = loading gain", not the zero-force point. R2 is even more direct: its own stored energy is small, its loading share is pushed onto the front legs that were already the heaviest, and the sum rises all the way with no usable falling signal.

Meaning for v2: total robot current is unfit as a per-leg zero-force detector, but adequate as residual telemetry — instead measure the current step at the instant of seal rupture (the rupture instant is given by the cup pressure venting→released transition; the vent valve coil was energized 0.4 s before rupture, so differencing cancels it automatically; 50 Hz per-tick sampling suffices); the step height ∝ residual stored energy, feeding directly into the per-leg correction rule of protocol §6 — from then on calibrating δ needs no camera. The endgame sensor is an IMU (the body acceleration pulse at rupture is an order of magnitude cleaner than bus current, and it also captures the true gravity direction of _down).

7. Landed and next steps

Landed (commit 2990449, 96 tests green + pty smoke on three branches):

  • parse_handover cap 25→45 (the old reason ">21 makes no sense beyond the bounce ceiling" came from the same wrong account as the "bounce×0.8" starting method); the workspace is guarded by climb_walk's hard refusal at startup (δmax ≤ comp_tail − half stride) + the step-4.7 per-tick envelope clipping — the smoke test confirms the budget account takes over correctly after the cap is lifted.
  • The --handover help of both scripts now describes the new calibration method; the design §5 calibration-semantics correction ⚠ is on record (the old table is annotated as archive only); the protocol §6 correction clamp 21→45, and the correction amount is now converted by the per-leg slope δ_i ← min(δ_i + 0.8×residual/|slope_i|, 45).
