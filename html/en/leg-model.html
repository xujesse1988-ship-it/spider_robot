R3 leg model: joint axes of the three printed parts, the assembly chain, and the leg-plane side view

This page has one purpose — to give "a spot on the leg" one unambiguous name. From now on, "20mm outboard of the knee axis" or "tibia part coordinates (54, −7.2)" means the same point to both of us. Every outline comes from an STL mesh projection, every joint axis from a circle fit to the STL hole walls, and the pose is driven by (γ, α, θ) from software/hexapod/kinematics.py.

All three axes are pinned down in their own part frames: coxa2 (3, −1)∥z and (46, 2.75)∥y, spacing 43.0 = coxa_len; femur (3,1)→(83,1), spacing 80.0.
Knee axis K = tibia part (54.0, −7.2) (centroid of the four flange holes, shifted 9.75 along the long axis); lip centre C = (1.3, −119), |K→C| = 123.6.
The side view is seen from the −Y side, i.e. the face the servo horns point towards — which is exactly the face images/leg.jpg was shot from. Every red line, red circle and green line in the photo has a counterpart in the drawing.

§1First, how to name a position

Two coordinate systems; always say which one. For everyday discussion use the leg frame; for printed-part detail (a particular hole, a particular face) use the part frame and name the part, e.g. "tibia part (54.0, −7.2)".

What the leg frame actually looks like

Three things about this frame tend to trip people up. One at a time.

  1. There is one frame per leg, not one for the whole machine. All six legs have their own origin and their own +X. A leg's +X is that leg's neutral direction (for R3, −125° in the body frame), which is a completely different thing from the body's +X (straight ahead).
  2. The origin is not on any part you can point at. First find the coxa yaw axis — the vertical axis line through the centre of the coxa servo horn; then travel 16.2mm up along it, to the height of the femur servo shaft. That point is the origin H. It is defined this way so that l1 is a purely horizontal 43mm (H→F has no vertical component), which keeps the kinematics as simple as possible.
  3. You do not have to memorise +Y. Remembering +X and +Z is enough; +Y = Z×X merely completes the right-handed set. For a right leg it points towards the front-outboard side of the body; in the side view of §2 it points into the page (because the viewpoint is at −Y).
Self-test: the coxa turns by γ while femur and tibia hold still — does F's X in the leg frame change?
It does. The leg frame is pinned to the body and does not turn with γ. As soon as γ moves, the whole leg (F, K, foot) sweeps a circle about the Z axis in that frame: F = (l1·cosγ, l1·sinγ, 0) — at γ=30° F's X shrinks from 43.00 to 37.24 while a Y component of 21.50 appears.

But: their horizontal distance r to the Z axis and their height z do not change at all. rF=l1, rK=l1+l2·cosα, zK=l2·sinα… depend only on α and θ, never on γ. So the side view in §2 is completely independent of γ — which is why it has no γ slider. In one sentence: γ rotates the leg plane itself; the shape inside that plane never moves.

(This is exactly what leg_joint_points in kinematics.py computes: first r and z from α and θ inside the leg plane, then multiply by cosγ / sinγ to spread them over X and Y.)

A few counter-intuitive points, up front. ① H is not on the servo-horn face — the z=0 plane of the leg frame passes through the femur axis, and the coxa horn seating face is 16.2mm below it. ② K is not at the tibia part origin; it is at (54.0, −7.2), settled on 2026-08-07. ③ The cup axis is not K→C; they differ by 25.2° — treating at as the cup direction is the old trap that biased CLIMBING-DESIGN by 22.7° across the board. ④ The two femur axes are exactly 80.0 apart in the STL, while config uses the measured 81.0 — a 1mm difference (see §6).

0°

The "neutral direction" that γ=0 refers to is mount_angle_deg in config, measured from body +X (forward), counter-clockwise positive. It comes from the second number on the MODE_STANDARD line of the official chica-config-2040.txt, "Corner leg angle 55"; the middle legs' 90° follows from symmetry. Note that the L1–R1 line is lateral (along body ±Y), not the X axis; it is the L1–L3 line that runs longitudinally (along body ±X).

A corner leg's neutral direction is not "radially outward from the body centre". The L1 hip (83.5, 63) sits at a polar angle of 37.0° about the body centre, while its neutral direction is 55° — the leg splays 18° further out than radial. The middle legs have no such offset (both are 90°). Adjacent legs' neutral directions are 35° apart (55→90→125), and mirror-image legs (L1–R1) 110° apart.

The consequence (default stance at foot_reach=130, computed): the L1 foot lands at (158.1, 169.5), which is 29mm further back and 28mm further out than the radial placement at (187.3, 141.3) — 40.6mm between the two points. Across the whole footprint, the front pair spans 339 laterally (radial: 283) while the front-to-back span is 316 (radial: 375) — wider but shorter, and the support polygon is in fact 9% smaller (1204 vs 1322 cm²). Nor do the six feet lie on one circle: corner feet sit 231.8 from the body centre, middle feet 211.5 — 20mm apart. So "stance radius" is not a meaningful single number for this machine; wall stances have to be worked out per leg.
⚠ mount_angle_deg is this package's definition, not a mechanical fact. The real mechanical direction depends on which spline tooth the horn was fitted to, and the difference is absorbed by the coxa's attach_deg — so it does not affect where the foot actually lands; what it does govern is the shape of the default stance and how well-centred the γ travel is. Measured (real direction at servo centre = mount_angle + attach_deg): L1 50.7°, R1 −54.6°, L2 91.5°, R2 −90.2° are all within ±4° of nominal, but L3 is 139.4° (nominal 125) and R3 is −145.4° (nominal −125) — both rear legs sit 14.4° and 20.4° further back, in fact closer to the radial ±143°. The consequence is that the rear legs' coxa travel is not centred (14~20° short on one side); worth checking the margin on those two legs before any large-sweep move.

Why the photo happens to show this face. On a right leg all three parts have their horns facing −Y: the coxa2 horn counterbore is in the lower jaw, both femur horn counterbores are on the −zpart side, and the tibia horn is on the +ypart side — once assembled they all face the same way, which is −Y. A side view drawn "X right, Z up" must be viewed from −Y, so the face it draws is the face where the horn screws are visible in the photo. Left-leg parts are mirrored and their horns face +Y.

§5Assembly chain: part coordinates → leg coordinates

Every point in the side view is computed this way. With at = α + θ − 180° and F = (l1 + 0, 0) + …, in detail:

The two "mirrors" are not typos: those two STLs are laid out in print orientation, so once fitted to the leg, looking from −Y you see their back side, and the planar mapping therefore carries one flip (determinant −1). For the tibia this flip is exactly where the minus sign in cup_delta_deg = −25.2 comes from — in part coordinates the square shaft is +25.24° counter-clockwise from K→C, and after assembly it becomes −25.24° clockwise.

Self-check: substitute α=35°, θ=68° (the official centred convention) and the cup axis comes out at at − 25.2° = −102.2°, only 12.2° off plumb — the cup lies almost flat on the ground, exactly what "a suction foot that walks on the floor" was designed to do. With the sign flipped it would be −51.8°, and the cup would jab the ground at 38°, clearly wrong.

§7Four things to know before using this

  1. 81 or 80. The design value of the part is 80.000 (the STL hole positions leave no room for doubt); config's 81.0 is the measured horn screw centre distance — the extra 1mm is the real stack-up of print tolerance plus horn/spline clearance. Using 81 in the kinematics is correct (it wants the actual axis distance), but do not mix the two when running interference checks against the STL.
  2. The z = 2.75 entry still carries ±1 of uncertainty. What it fixes is "the height of hip point H above the coxa horn face" (16.2mm). To close it: measure the vertical distance from the coxa horn seating face to the femur servo shaft centre; the prediction is 16.2±1. It affects neither l1/l2/l3 nor any kinematic number — the z=0 plane of the leg frame is defined on the femur axis in the first place.
  3. The cup has two states. This figure draws the free state (h_cup=19, l3=123.7). Once gripping, the bellows compacts, h_cup ≈ 7.5~8, effective l3 ≈ 113.8, and δ becomes 27.6°. P4 keeps its books as "free-state model throughout + press_delta"; do not mix the two conventions (P4-GUIDE §4.2).
  4. The hip spacings in config are not yet aligned with measurement. L1–R1 measures 125 (config 126) and L1–L3 measures 165 (config 167), with frame.stl giving 125.27 / 165.53 in agreement — those three official integers are rounded design intent, running 1~2mm large. Changing config means changing all three together (L2–R2 is not measured yet; expected ≈162.5), so mount_x/mount_y stay at 83.5 / 63.0 / 81.5 for now. Impact: each hip is off by 0.5~1mm, which ground gaits will never feel; wall stances are budgeted in mm, but this is far smaller than the elastic give of the leg chain (13~27mm), so it is not urgent.
  5. This figure is geometry, not mechanics. Under load the real leg chain gives elastically — press reaction can bend the diagonal front legs down by 13~27mm, and after mounting the wall the body sinks 6mm+26mm. To compute landing bands and stance margins, subtract the give after the geometry is done.
