"Leg gets dragged down before lifting" diagnosis — 2026-08-19 real climb on the glass door

Data: climb_20260819_184038.log (git f638e56, parameters --no-tank --sag-comp 8 --press-delta 20 --stand-height 62 --max-step 66 --speed 20 --tilt-trim 6) + real_climb.mp4 (720×1280@30fps, 52.6 s). Video time ≈ log time − 117.0 s (aligned by the two anchors of the R1/L1 upward swings). Pixel scale ≈1.05–1.15 mm/px (L2–R2 cup spacing 474.7 mm).

Conclusion: what you saw on R1 (video ≈20.0–22.0 s) and L1 (≈32.9–35.0 s), "the coxa first gets dragged down and then moves up", is neither part of the lift command nor cup slip — it is the reaction force of the previous leg (in both cases a rear leg: L3, R3) pressing down with press_delta=20, bending the leg-chain compliance of the five stance legs: the whole body sinks about 8 mm, and the front leg diagonal to the pressing leg (R1/L1) deforms the most, its mid-chain dropping 13–27 mm — exactly the "very large amplitude" you saw. Once attached and the press force removed it mostly springs back, and right after that the leg itself vents→lifts upward, which visually reads as "dragged down, then up". All 10 presses show a dip (front/middle legs too, 2–3× smaller and mixed with roll); in the gait order R3→L1→R2→L3→R1→L2, R1/L1 happen to follow the two rear legs and are their diagonals, hence the most conspicuous.

Evidence 1: cup seats do not move, body sinks — elastic give, not slip

NCC template tracking frame by frame (32.0–38.8 s, covering the R3-press and L1-press events): at the deepest point of the R3 press (32.9 s) the body's black line dives −7 px (≈8 mm) and then springs back, while the tips of the L3/R1/R3 cup seats stay within ±2 px throughout → the lips did not slide on the glass; all the sinking comes from leg-chain give (servo backlash/load deflection + bellows tilt). The front-leg L1 press (37.4 s) has a shallower dip mixed with a roll to the right (in the full curve of Evidence 2 there is a dip at every red line; a two-frame endpoint method skips short dips, so the first version of this report wrongly judged "front legs unaffected"; corrected after comparing with the floor video: all legs dip, rear legs deepest).

Evidence 2: every press has a dip, and the bottom of each dip is the instant before press detection

Vertical body tracking over the whole run (background-corrected; the vertical scale of this plot is compressed ~3× by the background lock, read the shape not the amplitude): red lines = press detected, blue dashed = vent. All 10 presses show a dip; the swing segments rise (clock advance + slip compensation), the wait-for-attachment segments are flat.

Counter-evidence: on the floor the coxa axis is vertical and gravity does not load coxa rotation, yet the floor video shows the same phenomenon (user comparison) → supports press-reaction rocking (on the floor it is seesaw-like: press one leg, the other corner sinks), not the gravity-lag hypothesis. Two good things confirmed along the way

① The vent 0.3 s design works: 30 fps close tracking of the R1 foot across vent/released (20.5–21.5 s): no rebound drop at the release instant (±1 px), then straight into the upward swing. Residual vacuum dragging the foot is gone.

② press 20 on glass attaches every time: all 10 landings attached on the first try, reaching −61 to −68 kPa after 0.3 s of pumping, zero retries / zero threshold waits / zero leak rescues. So 20 mm of press on glass is far more than needed — exactly the margin that can be dialed back.
