Single-leg calibration: which angle θ / α / γ is, and how to measure it

All three joint angles are angles between virtual lines and a reference (another line / gravity / the body), not between physical part edges. θ is done (86.4° in config); α of the femur and γ of the coxa remain (§04, §05).

F

femur horn center screw (hip pitch axis). The center of the center screw on the femur servo output shaft.

K

tibia horn center screw (knee axis). §3.4 confirmed horn center = knee axis.

P

Center of the cup lip (free state, no vacuum). The measured KP is at the same time tibia_len under this convention.

01Measuring θ: three sides fix the angle, no protractor ✅ done

  1. Run software/scripts/servo_calib_helper.py, type 17 1510 (L1 tibia to the calibration center; the script defaults to 1500 at startup and must be changed to 1510 manually).
  2. Channels 15/16 (coxa, femur) just need to be swung somewhere convenient for the calipers — F and K are both on the femur rigid body, so θ is set by the knee servo alone. Keep enabled throughout; never bend by hand.
  3. Measure three point-to-point straight distances: ① FK (should be ≈80 mm, as a self-check) ② KP ③ FP. Screw center to screw center, measure twice more and average.
θ = arccos( (FK² + KP² − FP²) / (2 · FK · KP) )

Error reference: 1 mm of side length ≈ 1° of angle. More than enough to judge the 44° reference question; to fill config (±1–2°) measure carefully with calipers.

02Do not measure it like this

  • A protractor against any physical edge of segment A or B — θ is the angle of the virtual line K→P relative to K→F, and there is very likely no face on the part parallel to K→P (§4.2).
  • Treating "segment A in line with the femur" as the θ=180° reference — θ in that pose is actually 180°−δ′ (δ′ = the fixed angle between segment A's direction and K→P).
  • Using the square shaft / square box side as a reference face — the square-shaft direction differs from K→P by about 20° (δ in the figure, §4.1).

04femur: α_center by height difference ✅ result: 49.7°

Measured 2026-07-17 Δh = h_K − h_F = 61 mm → α_center = arcsin(61/80) = 49.7°, landing right on the official 35° + one spline tooth 14.4° = 49.4° (0.3° off); in config. Setup figure and method kept for the record.

Align the terms first (labeled in the figure):

  • L1 = the whole left-front leg (coxa + femur + tibia-suction one-piece part), L=left, 1=front, channels 15/16/17. Not a point.
  • Hip = the two axes at the leg root: C (coxa axis, vertical, horizontal swing) + F (femur axis, horizontal, lift). The "hip" in "knee above the hip / cup below the hip" means F.
  • Knee = K (tibia horn center screw); cup center = P. The same points as in the θ three-side method.
α_center = arcsin( (h_K − h_F) / 80 ) (K higher is positive; 80 = measured FK)
  1. Body flat (phone level ≤0.3°), L1 overhanging the edge but with F and K both still above the table (as in the figure, only the cup past the edge). Type 16 1510, enabled throughout.
  2. Measure table → top of F screw, table → top of K screw. Same screw type, so the head radius cancels in the difference. Precision: ±0.5 mm → ±0.6°.
  3. Cross-check: the femur has a long straight edge verified parallel to F→K (§2.5); zero the level on the body first, then read against that edge; should agree with the height method (within 1°).
  4. How to fill it in: femur attach_deg := α_center; robot.py passes α directly, no reference conversion. Expected 35 ± n·14.4°.

05coxa: γ_center by top-view projection ⏸ deferred to P3

Decision 2026-07-17: defer to P3, when the body is assembled. In P0 there is no body, so the zero of γ (neutral direction 55°) does not physically exist; the P1/P2 single-leg rig does not depend on the absolute γ (pressure closed loop; the error is only a lateral shift of ~2.3 mm/°); in P3 use the "re-check version" baseline method at the end directly (does not rely on the pocket-alignment assumption, more robust). The servo-case reference method below is kept as a fallback — if lateral aiming turns out to be off during P2, it can be used any time for a provisional value.

Re-check version with the body on (for the record) — uses the line between the projected L1↔R1 horn screws as the reference, independent of the "pocket aligned to 55°" assumption. Not usable now without a body:

  1. coxa servo case bottom flat on the table and clamped or weighted down (the leg is a cantilever and will pull an unfixed case askew), graph paper pressed under the case. Type 15 1510.
  2. Reference line: trace a straight line on the paper along the long edge of the case — parallel to the case axis, this is the 0° reference for γ; positive direction toward the output-shaft end.
  3. Leg direction line: coxa fixed, swing femur/tibia to two different extensions, at each project the cup lip center vertically onto the paper with a square and mark it; join the two points (same point discipline as θ/femur).
  4. Take far points on the paper, read coordinates and compute the angle between the two lines with atan2 (do not use a small protractor): γ_center = angle of the leg line relative to the case axis, counter-clockwise positive in top view. Expected −8 ± n·14.4°. Fill coxa attach_deg := γ_center.
  5. Premise and re-check: this method assumes the body pocket aligns the case axis with the neutral direction 55° (official design intent). Once the body is on, re-check with the "re-check version" baseline method above; if not urgent, the whole measurement can also be deferred to body assembly — the single-leg rig (P1/P2) does not use γ. Once all three attach values are in, do an FK spot check (send joint angles, measure the foot vs. leg_fk, pass at <5 mm).
